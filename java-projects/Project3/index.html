01 · Online privacy

Know what you share

Online privacy is about how information about you is collected, used, stored, and shared. Understanding the choices an app offers helps you make informed decisions—not just tap “allow” by habit.

◎

What might apps collect?

Depending on what an app does and its settings, it may request your name, email address, age, contacts, photos, device identifiers, search or usage activity, and location. Information can also be inferred from how and when you use a service.

⌖

Location, in simple terms

A phone can estimate where it is using signals such as GPS satellites, nearby Wi-Fi networks, and cell towers. An app with permission may use that estimate to show directions or nearby places. Background access can allow location updates even when the app is not open.

◇

Why request permissions?

Permissions can enable useful features: navigation needs location, a photo editor may need selected photos, and a messaging app might offer contact matching. Sometimes a request is broader than a feature requires, so check the reason and the app's privacy information.

✓

Protect your privacy

Review permissions and privacy settings regularly. Choose access only when needed, prefer “while using” or selected-item access when available, and remove permissions you no longer want. Ask a trusted adult for help if a setting or request is unclear.

02 · Computing laws

Laws shape software

Software developers need to consider the rules that apply to the service, the information it handles, and the people it serves. Laws can create responsibilities for both how a product is designed and how it operates.

§

COPPA · United States jurisdiction

The Children's Online Privacy Protection Act (COPPA) is a United States federal law. COPPA and its implementing rule impose requirements on covered websites and online services that collect personal information from children under 13.

Certain covered services must give notice and obtain verifiable parental consent before collecting personal information from children, subject to applicable exceptions. COPPA does not apply identically to every website or every user; whether a service is covered depends on the law and facts about that service.

Developers need to understand applicable laws early so they can build appropriate notices, consent flows, data practices, and safeguards into software. Legal requirements can vary by jurisdiction and situation, so product teams may need qualified legal guidance.

Why it matters: understanding the rules before launch can help prevent harmful data practices and avoid noncompliance. This overview is educational, not legal advice.

03 · Technology ethics

Build with people in mind

Ethics helps developers think about what they should do—not only what they can do. Performance Standard 5.3.4 asks us to discuss the role of ethics in technologies, including how design choices affect people's rights, safety, and trust.

Privacy & informed consent

Respect people's personal information. Informed consent means people can understand what they are agreeing to, why information is needed, and what choices they have.

Transparency

Explain data practices clearly and honestly: what is collected, how it is used, and whether it is shared. Important details should not be hidden in confusing settings or language.

Fairness

Consider who benefits and who could be harmed. Test for unequal impacts, avoid unnecessary barriers, and treat people respectfully across different circumstances.

Responsible data collection

Collect only what is needed for a clear purpose, protect it, and avoid keeping it longer than necessary. More data can create more risk without adding real value.

Ethical responsibility can extend beyond minimum legal requirements. A practice might be technically allowed but still surprise, pressure, or disadvantage users. Responsible design considers trust and human impact, too.
